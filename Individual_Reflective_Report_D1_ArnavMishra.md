# Individual Reflective Report — Deliverable 1

My main contribution was leading the research direction through the literature review, drafting initial deliverables, refining documents, clarifying requirements with the module lead, and coordinating team follow-ups.

We applied the empathise and define stages of design thinking to explore existing solutions and understand stakeholder needs. Interview transcripts, personas, scenarios, thematic analysis, and latent ideas helped us challenge assumptions and translate stakeholder perspectives into evidence-informed epics. This established a foundation for later stages of the software development life cycle (SDLC).

Our iterative, Agile-like process involved circulating drafts, peer review, and revision. Cross-checking aligned the literature review, stakeholder analysis, and prioritised epics, improving consistency across deliverables.

One challenge was that drafting early risked allowing my initial framing to influence decisions before sufficient discussion. Peer review helped strengthen our shared direction, although agreed review criteria could have made feedback more systematic.

In future, I would introduce a shared, rubric-aligned checklist and assign clear revision ownership. Comparing successive drafts against the rubric would help assess coherence, completeness, and conciseness. I learned that effective research leadership requires balancing initiative with collaborative scrutiny and grounding design decisions in evidence.
