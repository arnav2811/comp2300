# D1 Individual Reflection — Hiranya Makkar

I led the first-pass coding of CF5–CF7 and helped review the full interview set. These accounts connected delivery packaging, household travel and landlord-paid electricity with practical constraints. My contribution helped connect everyday examples to the wider themes rather than treating each activity as an isolated feature request.

A key analytical difficulty was distinguishing visible consumption from responsibility. CF7's landlord-paid electricity suggested that showing a bill alone might not motivate shared action. Reading this alongside other accounts supported the latent theme of diffused responsibility in shared housing. Thematic analysis was useful because it moved the interpretation beyond topic labels, but the resulting themes remain interpretations of illustrative coursework interviews, not representative findings about Indian users.

The main improvement is to make that reasoning easier to audit. For D2, I will maintain a shared traceability table linking each proposed story to its supporting code, theme and scenario, while recording contradictory evidence. I will also distinguish automation that checks consistency from interpretation requiring human judgement. This should reduce repetitive checking without allowing a polished output to hide unsupported assumptions.
