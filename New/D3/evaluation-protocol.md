# D3 evaluation plan — working protocol

**Status:** Proposed protocol; no recruitment, participant session or result exists.

## Aim

Evaluate whether the selected prototype helps a user understand a recorded
activity, inspect estimate provenance, recognise constraints and distinguish a
shared total from an individual attribution. The evaluation does not claim to
measure actual carbon reduction.

## Method

Use moderated task-based usability testing with think-aloud prompts, conducted in
a quiet controlled room. The official specification requires participants from
another COMP2300 group, recruited by the team, and prohibits sensitive personal
data. The final protocol must be checked against any lecturer clarification.

## Recruitment and ethics boundary

- Target: participants from one other COMP2300 group; exact sample size requires
  team confirmation and a justified fallback rule.
- Recruit through an agreed course channel; participation is voluntary.
- Do not collect names in the analysis file, demographic profiles, health data,
  political/religious beliefs, financial details or real household records.
- Use synthetic activity values during tasks.
- Prepare an information sheet, consent script, withdrawal explanation and
  de-identified observation form before any session.

## Tasks

| ID | Participant task | Observable success |
|---|---|---|
| T1 | Record a home-electricity activity and explain the result | Enters valid quantity; identifies scope and numeric status |
| T2 | Inspect the estimate assumptions | Finds factor/formula; identifies illustrative or verified status |
| T3 | Mark a safety/shift-constrained journey and choose an action | Protected choice is retained; participant identifies why advice is constrained |
| T4 | Record packaging or repair/reuse | Understands that a descriptive record does not imply an invented carbon credit |
| T5 | Inspect a shared household total | States that it does not establish one person's share |

## Measures

Record task completion, critical errors, requests for help, time only where the
observer can record it consistently, comprehension answers and brief comments.
Use a fixed observation schema. Avoid significance testing for a small convenience
sample unless justified by the actual data and module guidance.

## Proposed success criteria for team approval

Before recruitment, the team should either adopt or amend this explicit rule:

- target sample: 5 participants; minimum interpretable sample: 3;
- task success means the participant completes the task without a critical error;
- a comprehension response is recorded separately from completion and help;
- proposed task gate: at least 2/3, 3/4 or 4/5 successful observations when the
  corresponding sample size is obtained, with no critical issue for the task;
- a sample outside 3–5 is reported as outside the planned gate rather than
  automatically passing;
- incomplete/missing rows remain visible in the denominator and are not converted
  to success or zero;
- no participant is required to disclose sensitive data;
- findings remain descriptive and cannot establish carbon reduction.

These are proposed thresholds, not results. The team must approve them and record
the decision before collecting data.

## Analysis plan

1. Validate the observation file and document missing rows.
2. Calculate task completion/error counts with explicit denominators.
3. Summarise time descriptively if enough valid observations exist.
4. Code comments by issue type: comprehension, provenance, constraint handling,
   shared scope, accessibility and interaction friction.
5. Triangulate observations with task outcomes; do not treat opinion as measured
   behaviour change.
6. Report sample, setting, moderator influence, prototype fidelity and limitations.

## Required materials before D3 submission

Information sheet, consent script, recruitment message, task cards,
post-task questions, observer form, de-identification procedure, analysis schema,
data-retention decision and an actual pilot review record.
